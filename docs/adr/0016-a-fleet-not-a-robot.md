# The first Universe is a fleet around a monolith, because that is what free models can build

Status: accepted

Suptron is named after a combining robot and its vocabulary follows that metaphor (ADR-0004), so a reader would expect the Universe to show robots. We decided that the first Universe is deep space: the Core is a dark monolith, every Slot is a dock with a ship, a Sub-pilot is a drone, a Side Slot is a small shuttle beside its dock, a Loop is an arc of energy and an Exit Point is a gate. The reason is the models. Nobody on the project draws 3D, the repository is public, so only models whose licence allows committing them (CC0, CC-BY) are used, and a search on 2026-11-20 found that space is the only theme where every part exists and fits together.

## Considered Options

- **A monolith and a fleet (chosen)**: two consistent sets of ten and twelve distinct ships, a station, ring gates, dock platforms and drones, all CC0 or CC-BY. Which exact set is used is decided in the PLAN, with the models on screen.
- **A hangar of robots**: closest to the vocabulary, and a Mission could end with the robots combining. The largest consistent free set has sixteen robots, in a bright toy style, with no animations; no free model transforms between a vehicle and a robot; the well-made ones are fan copies of trademarked characters. Rejected for the first Universe, kept as a candidate for a later one.
- **A desert outpost in the spirit of Dune**: the best mood (brutalist monoliths, sandstone, monumental gates), but nothing original to stand in the docks: every good ornithopter and sandworm is a copy of the film's design. The same ships on landing pads among dunes would work and is the natural next world.
- **Paid model packs**: none of the licences read allows committing model files to a public repository. A model the Commander wants badly enough is discussed as its own case.

## Consequences

- No free pack has the monumental, muted look the Commander wants, so the look comes from the scene: models are recoloured to one muted palette, with fog and sparse lights, and colour is kept for meaning (done, failed, waiting for the Commander).
- Ships do not move on their own, unlike rigged robots; life comes from lights, engine glow and slow hovering.
- CC-BY models oblige the repository to name their authors.
- Each Unit gets its ship automatically from its name, so the same Unit always looks the same and the Unit's format does not change; with ten or twelve ships, two Units of a large Hangar may share one.
